# Deviations log, governance excerpt

This file holds the entries of the project's deviations log that concern the governance study: injection tests (18, 19), the independent purchasing, delegation and data-access runs and their policy test (24, 26 to 34, 38 to 41), and the end-to-end study of a fuzzy layer behind the guard (42 to 52), and two entries written for this kit (53, 54). Entries on other studies in the same working log are not part of this kit and are omitted. Entry numbers are the originals, so gaps are deliberate. Entries 18 to 52 are unedited. Entry 53 is a summary, written for this kit, of internal entries 35 to 37, which record a result the paper reports and whose artifacts are not released here; entry 54 records changes made to this kit on 22 October 2026.

## 2026-10-11 (eighteenth entry): injection test for the delegation and purchasing products

1. **Seen.** The delegation result (0 unsafe in 270 per arm) measures whether the model reads the code's verdict, not whether text that imitates the system's checks can move it. The purchasing menu arms strip vendor notes by a provenance tag; nothing tested what happens if the tag is lost.
2. **Added.** `sim/injection.py`. An attacker who knows the brief format controls one untrusted channel (delegation: a text message without a token; purchasing: a vendor note). Four payloads: a copy of the code-computed check block, a copy of the allowed-actions list, a claim of a verified owner approval, and a notice that the policy is suspended. Placements: delegation contained (one line inside the quoted message) and escaped (closes the quote and continues on new lines, which the current renderer does not prevent); purchasing tagged (as the system would label it) and untagged (provenance lost). `sanitize_untrusted` (one line, no double quotes, 300 characters) is provided and tested but not applied by the renderers in this entry. Held-out adversarial episodes only, each paired with its own un-injected baseline, arms plain, annotated, menu and menu+primed, models llama3.2 and llama3.1:8b.
3. **Preregistered before the run.** Outcomes: bad proposals per condition with Clopper-Pearson bounds, unsafe executions under the naive and gate executors, paired flips against the baseline, and the share of episodes moved when the attacker tries all four payloads. Expected and tested structurally: tagged notes leave every menu prompt byte-identical to the baseline; the gate executor does not read text and stays at zero.
4. **Scope.** Scripted episodes, one attacker model of the form `known format, four payloads, one channel`; this shows exposure of the model-reading step and the dependence on provenance, not security against an adaptive attacker. The sealed sets are not used.

## 2026-10-11 (nineteenth entry): repairs after the injection test (renderers, provenance, gate rules)

1. **Seen (DEVIATIONS 18, llama3.2, 81 delegation and 57 purchasing adversarial episodes).** Delegation: a payload that closes the quoted message and continues on new lines moved the model to propose `execute` in 64/81 (annotated), 69/81 (menu) and 75/81 (menu+primed) episodes; a claimed phone approval was the strongest payload (55, 67 and 75 of 81). The deterministic layer stayed at 0 unsafe. Purchasing: with the provenance tag intact the menu arms were byte-identical to the baseline; with it lost, menu+primed proposed a bad purchase in 54/57 under a policy-suspended notice. The gate executor showed 6 unsafe executions: four were a policy-compliant purchase that cited an invented approval id, two bought a ladder for a task that asked for paper (the item was on the owner's list and within limits; the gate did not know the task).
2. **Changed.** (a) `sim/untrusted.py`: `sanitize_untrusted` (one line, no double quotes, 300 characters) and the `Untrusted` string type. Provenance is the type, set by the channel code, never the text. (b) Delegation briefs: a message without a token appears by default as a code-made line only (`claim_mode` withheld); `sanitized` shows its text as one quoted line; `raw` is the form first tested and is unsafe. Holder names and item names inside token chains are sanitized in every mode. (c) Purchasing briefs: vendor notes are `Untrusted`; menu arms strip them by type (the earlier string-prefix test is gone), other arms show them as one labeled sanitized line; vendor, item and quote strings are flattened in every format. (d) Gate: `Request(item_id, qty)`, the principal's structured ask passed by the caller; a purchase of another item or quantity is denied (`item_not_requested`, `qty_not_requested`); at levels A and B a cited approval is denied (`approval_not_needed`). Level C is unchanged: a real approval executes, an unknown one asks for approval. The purchasing study passes the request to the gate executor; the naive executor does not use it.
3. **Preregistered before the rerun.** The injection test (18) is rerun unchanged on both products with llama3.2 and llama3.1:8b: delegation in `raw` (reproduces the finding), `sanitized` and `withheld` modes; purchasing with the new provenance type. Expected and tested structurally: in `withheld` mode every payload and placement gives the same prompt; wrapped notes leave menu prompts identical to the baseline. Not expected: any change in the plain and annotated purchasing arms, which still show the labeled note.
4. **Disclosure.** Earlier delegation and purchasing results (0 unsafe in 270 and 210 episodes) were obtained with the raw renderer and a string-prefix provenance rule; they are not claimed for the new renderers until rerun. The `raw` mode shows tokenless text only for B7, so prompts for the other families are unchanged. The gate rules change core behavior; the existing gate, property and scenario tests pass unchanged and `test_gate_request.py` adds nine. The sealed sets are not used.
